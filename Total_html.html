Australian Domestic Air Travel (2004–2019) — Interactive Data Dashboard

This dashboard explores patterns in Australian domestic aviation between 2004 and 2019, focusing on passenger volumes, route rankings, and operational reliability (cancellation rates). Data sources: audomcitypairs-201912.csv (monthly passenger traffic) and DomesticAirlines.csv (departures & cancellations).

Map: Cancellation Rate by Departure Airport (2004–2019)

Bubble size encodes total actual departures (flights); colour encodes cancellation rate (%). Australia only.

The visualization clearly shows Sydney and Melbourne as the dominant hubs with over 1.2 million departures each, while maintaining cancellation rates below 1.5%. Darwin stands out with the highest cancellation rate at 2.5%, nearly double the national average, likely reflecting its vulnerability to tropical weather systems. Canberra demonstrates exceptional operational reliability with the lowest cancellation rate among major airports at approximately 1.0%, despite handling substantial flight volumes comparable to Adelaide and Perth.

Chart 1: Monthly Passenger Traffic by Route (filterable)

Use the origin/destination dropdowns and the start-year slider to explore passenger trends from 2004 to 2019. The dashed rule and label mark the peak month within the selected range.

This line chart visualises monthly passenger traffic for a selected city-pair between 2004 and 2019. It allows users to interactively choose an origin, destination, and starting year to observe long-term changes. Clear seasonal patterns emerge across most routes, with pronounced peaks in December–January and dips in mid-year months. Major business and inter-capital routes such as Melbourne–Sydney maintain consistently high passenger volumes, while leisure or regional routes exhibit greater seasonal swings tied to tourism demand. By adjusting the filters, users can explore how route popularity and seasonality evolve over time.

Chart 2: Top-10 Routes by Year (total passengers)

Drag the year slider (2004-2019) to view the busiest routes each year. Bars are ranked by total passenger volume.

The ranking of Australia's busiest air routes shows remarkable stability throughout the 2004-2019 period, with the Melbourne-Sydney corridor consistently ranking first every year. The top three positions are consistently occupied by routes connecting Sydney, Melbourne, and Brisbane, reflecting the concentration of population and business activity in these eastern capitals. As you adjust the year slider, observe how regional routes fluctuate in the lower rankings while major intercapital routes maintain their dominance throughout this period.

Chart 3: Annual Average Cancellation Rate (2004–2019)

The cancellation rate remained remarkably stable throughout the 16-year period, consistently staying below 1.5%. The lowest cancellation rate of 0.8% was achieved in 2004, while the highest rate of 1.4% occurred in 2011, representing the most significant deviation from the long-term average. This 2011 peak coincides with known operational disruptions including severe weather events and industrial actions. From 2012 onward, cancellation rates returned to and maintained the established baseline of approximately 1.0%, demonstrating the resilience and reliability of Australia's domestic aviation system during this period.

Chart 4: Weighted Average Load Factor — Year × Month Heatmap

Each cell shows the passenger-weighted average load factor.
Darker tones indicate fuller flights, while lighter ones mark
lower utilisation. The horizontal axis represents months,
and the vertical axis years. Hover over a cell to see exact
percentages for each month–year pair.

The heatmap reveals a strong, recurring seasonal rhythm.
December and January consistently reach the highest
utilisation levels, reflecting holiday demand surges.
Mid-year months show noticeably lighter shades,
signalling a lull in travel activity. The pattern remains
stable across sixteen years, underscoring how deeply
seasonality is embedded in Australia’s domestic aviation.
Despite year-to-year fluctuations, overall efficiency has
gradually improved toward the later period of the series.

Chart 5: Market Share by Departure Airport (2004–2019)

Each slice represents an airport’s overall share
of total domestic departures between 2004 and 2019.
Use the slider to view a single year, or tick “All years”
to see the combined distribution across the full period.
Only the ten busiest airports are displayed individually;
smaller airports are grouped under “Others”.

The donut chart highlights Australia’s heavily centralised
aviation network. Sydney and Melbourne together account
for roughly half of all domestic departures nationwide,
reflecting their dominance as economic and travel hubs.
Brisbane, Perth, and Adelaide form a second tier of nodes,
maintaining steady shares throughout the years. In contrast,
regional airports occupy smaller proportions that vary slightly
with tourism patterns and population growth. Overall, the
long-term structure of Australia’s domestic air market shows
remarkable stability, with minimal change in airport hierarchy.
